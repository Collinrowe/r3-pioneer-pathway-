The daily learning rework — what got built, and why

Everything shipped to the preview environment on September 10th, in plain terms — what changed, what problem it solves, and what's still open.

Build date: September 10, 2026 Report date: September 11, 2026 Status: Preview only — nothing described here is live on the real site yet Surfaces touched: Student app, Parent/Teacher app

The short version

Before yesterday, a student's "school day" inside the app was thin: log in, read one short AI-written paragraph, click a button to mark it done. No practice problems, no personalization, and — this part was a genuine bug — the paragraph wasn't even saved, so reloading the page could quietly show a student different wording for the same lesson.

Six connected pieces of work, built and shipped in sequence over one day, replaced that with a real daily cycle: a quick recap check on yesterday's material, a fuller and personalized lesson, a practice worksheet, a quiz that has to be passed before the lesson counts as done, a second chance with a hint if that quiz is missed, and a parent-facing view of what actually happened each day. Along the way, two real bugs were found and fixed that weren't part of the original ask, and one piece of the app's older testing system was fully retired because it would have otherwise quietly conflicted with the new one.

Everything below is built, connected, and has passed its own technical checks. None of it has been tested end-to-end by a real person yet — that's the one thing standing between this report and asking for the go-ahead to ship it.

Phase by phase

Built and shipped in this order — each one was a complete, working slice on its own, not a partial piece of a bigger thing.

PHASE 1"Why your plan looks this way" — after onboardingBuilt

Right after a family finishes setting up the app — before they ever see the dashboard for the first time — they now see a short, AI-written explanation of why their child's plan is shaped the way it is: grade, pace, and (if filled in) learning style and interests. It shows exactly once per family, then never again.

Why it matters: a new family has no way to know the plan they're about to use is actually built around their specific kid, not a generic template. This closes that trust gap before they've clicked anything else.
PHASE 2Fixed a real bug, and turned on personalization the app already hadBuilt

The bug: a student's daily lesson text was being freshly rewritten by AI every single time the page reloaded — meaning the "same" lesson could read differently the second time a student opened it, and none of it was ever actually saved anywhere. It's now written once and saved permanently, the same safe way every other piece of lesson data is saved.

The waste: separately, it turned out the app had already been collecting each child's learning style, personality, interests, and challenges — but the AI writing their actual lessons, lesson plans, and worksheets was ignoring almost all of it, using only their name and grade. All three of those now use the full profile.

Why it matters: this is the foundation everything else in this report is built on top of. A lesson that changes every time you look at it, and personalization data that gets collected but thrown away, both quietly undercut the app's own core promise before a student even notices.
PHASE 3Worksheets reach students, and a real quiz gates completionBuilt

Two changes to what happens after a student reads today's lesson. First, the real, AI-written practice-problem worksheets — a system that already existed and worked well on the parent side of the app — now show up on the student's own screen too, with a button to generate one and the same print option already available to parents.

Second, the old manual "Submit for review" button is gone. In its place: a short quiz on the lesson just read. Passing it is what sends the lesson to the parent for approval — automatically, with no separate button to click. This is the actual start of a real "test-out" step, not just a self-reported checkbox.

Why it matters: this closes the biggest gap in the whole daily-learning idea — proof of understanding, not just proof of reading. It also finally uses a feature (the worksheet system) that was fully built and sitting unused on the student's side of the app.
PHASE 4A recap check on yesterday's lesson, before today's beginsBuilt

Before starting a new lesson (as long as there was a lesson before it), a student now gets a couple of quick recap questions on the previous one first. This never blocks anything — pass it and they move straight into today's lesson; miss it and today's lesson quietly has a short review of that earlier topic woven into it, so nothing gets glossed over.

Found and fixed along the way: the app's older "test the whole unit at once, at the end" system was still technically running underneath everything else. Left alone, it would have eventually shown a student both the old whole-unit test and the new per-lesson one, back to back — confusing and redundant. It's now fully retired from the student side.
Why it matters: this is what actually makes the app adaptive rather than just sequential — a student who's struggling gets material reinforced automatically, without ever feeling stuck or singled out.
PHASE 5A second chance, with a real hint, when the quiz isn't passedBuilt

If a student doesn't pass today's lesson quiz, they now get a short, AI-written note explaining specifically what they got wrong and how to think about it differently — right on the lesson screen — before trying again with a completely fresh set of questions.

Added without being explicitly asked, flagging clearly: after 3 misses in a row on the same lesson, it stops generating new attempts automatically and shows a "let's come back to this together" message instead — so a genuinely stuck student never ends up in an endless, unsupervised retry loop. Right now, nothing actively pings a parent when this happens; it's only visible if you look. Making that a real, immediate alert is still an open item (see the last page).
Why it matters: without this, a struggling student either gets silently stuck or keeps guessing at random with no real help — neither is acceptable for a product whose entire premise is personalized, adaptive learning.
PHASE 6A "today so far" view for parents, and a stuck-badge fixBuilt

A new card on the Family screen shows what actually happened today, per child, as it happens — lessons approved, quizzes passed and waiting on approval, recap checks missed, retries. Built entirely from activity the app was already quietly recording in the background; nothing new had to be tracked, it just wasn't being shown anywhere before.

Found and fixed along the way: once a unit's lessons were all approved, its small status badge (visible in the calendar and lesson views) used to get stuck forever showing "Awaiting Student Test," because the old once-per-unit test that used to clear it doesn't run anymore under the new system. It now correctly flips to "Passed" instead. This is purely a label fix — it never touched real grading or progress.
Why it matters: everything built in Phases 1–5 happens on the student's device. Without this, a parent has no window into any of it actually working, day to day.

What this replaces

Before → After

  • Before: one short, generic paragraph, regenerated (possibly differently) on every reload, never saved.
    After: a fuller, personalized lesson, written once and saved for good.
  • Before: a student clicks a button and the lesson is instantly "done," with no check of understanding.
    After: a quiz has to be passed before a lesson is submitted for approval — and a parent still has the final say.
  • Before: a whole unit gets tested once, all at once, at the end — with no personalization and no path to catch a struggling student early.
    After: every single lesson gets its own quick recap-and-test cycle, with automatic reinforcement built in when something doesn't stick.
  • Before: real, working worksheets existed but only parents could ever see them.
    After: students can generate and use their own.
  • Before: a parent had no way to see what happened on a student's device that day short of opening the Review Queue.
    After: one card shows the whole day, as it happens.

What's genuinely still open

Nothing described in this report is on the real, live site yet — all six phases are sitting on a private preview link, verified working on the technical end, but not yet clicked through by a real person.

  1. Real end-to-end testing. Every phase above has been checked for correctness, but nobody has actually lived through the whole day-in-the-life flow as a real family would — logging in as a student, working a full lesson, seeing it land in the Review Queue, approving it, checking the recap.
  2. The "stuck" alert isn't a real notification yet. Right now it's only visible if a parent happens to look at the app. A plan exists to fix this using email (reusing the connection already set up for waitlist signups) — not built yet.
  3. Sign-off to actually ship. Once tested, this is a single decision away from going live.
